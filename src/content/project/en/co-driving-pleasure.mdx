## Research lens & approach

![Research lens and method structure](/images/work/co-driving/research-lens.webp)

- **Theoretical lens**  
  Phenomenology and Technological Mediation, focusing on how technologies shape human perception, action, and experience.

- **Methods**  
  Trip Experience Sampling and Phenomenological Interviews conducted in real-world driving contexts.

- **Analytical focus**  
  Lived experience, co-driving patterns, and the emergence of pleasure in human–vehicle interaction.

---

## Key insights

### Pleasure is relational, not functional
Co-driving pleasure does not arise from isolated vehicle features, but from the dynamic relationship between human intentions and vehicle agency.

### Trust acts as a threshold for co-driving pleasure
Pleasure only emerges when trust in the vehicle’s behavior is established and sustained over time.

### Pleasure takes multiple forms in co-driving
Two major forms and five sub-types of pleasure were identified across different co-driving situations and contexts.

## Design frameworks & models

### Human–Vehicle Relation (HVR) Framework

![Human–Vehicle Relation Framework](/images/work/co-driving/hvr-framework.webp)

A conceptual framework describing how human intentions, vehicle agency, and contextual factors interact to shape different forms of human–vehicle relations in co-driving situations.  
The framework helps designers reason about experience beyond interface-level interactions, focusing instead on relational dynamics.

---

### Co-Driving Pleasure Model

![Co-Driving Pleasure Model](/images/work/co-driving/pleasure-model.webp)

A design-oriented model translating experiential insights into actionable levers that influence the emergence and quality of co-driving pleasure.  
It connects contextual needs, interaction patterns, and experiential outcomes in a coherent structure that can inform design decisions.

---

## Design implications

- Designing *with* vehicle agency rather than against it  
- Supporting negotiated control instead of binary automation  
- Attending to experiential continuity across driving situations  
- Treating pleasure as an experiential quality shaped over time, not a momentary feature

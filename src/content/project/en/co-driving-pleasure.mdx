## Key insights

### Pleasure is relational, not functional
Co-driving pleasure does not arise from isolated vehicle features, but from the dynamic relationship between human intentions and vehicle agency.

### Trust acts as a threshold for co-driving pleasure
Pleasure only emerges when trust in the vehicle’s behavior is established and sustained over time.

### Pleasure takes multiple forms in co-driving
Two major forms and five sub-types of pleasure were identified across different co-driving situations and contexts.

## Design implications

- Designing *with* vehicle agency rather than against it  
- Supporting negotiated control instead of binary automation  
- Attending to experiential continuity across driving situations  
- Treating pleasure as an experiential quality shaped over time, not a momentary feature

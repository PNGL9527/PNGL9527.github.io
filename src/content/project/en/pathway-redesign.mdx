## One pathway, different perspectives

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pathway-redesign/boundary-object-cover.webp" alt="Conceptual illustration of designers and healthcare professionals discussing a shared care pathway" caption="Two professional perspectives, one shared object of discussion. Conceptual illustration, not an application screenshot." />

Improving a care service requires more than one kind of knowledge. A service designer may notice confusing information or a difficult encounter; a clinical manager may focus on waiting time or resource use. **How can these concerns be discussed around the same process?**

Pathway Redesign uses the clinical pathway as a shared reference. It explores the role of a **boundary object**: something different professional groups can work around without needing to share the same expertise.

| Perspective | What it brings into the conversation |
| --- | --- |
| Experience design | Arrangements, communication, encounters, locations, and qualitative observations. |
| Shared pathway | The care sequence, decision points, branches, and selected patient flow. |
| Clinical management | Lead time, waiting time, costs, effectiveness, and related evidence. |

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pathway-redesign/workspace.png" alt="Actual Pathway Redesign web prototype with red experience-design fields, a central yellow clinical pathway, and blue clinical-managerial fields" caption="The actual web prototype: two professional perspectives connected to a shared clinical pathway." />

## Follow a flow, then compare the evidence

The workspace keeps both perspectives connected to the same selected sub-section and patient flow. Observations and measurements sit beside their interpretation, rather than being reduced to a score alone.

1. **Choose a point in the pathway.** Select a sub-section and flow to focus the discussion.
2. **Record what each perspective sees.** Add observations, roles, places, operational measures, and reasons for any grades.
3. **Read across the evidence.** Use the matrix to compare flows and identify questions or priorities for redesign.

## From mapping to dialogue

The project began with a service blueprint of Humanitas's pre-admission process during my master's work. Its representation developed through three forms:

- **Service blueprint:** understand the service process and its encounters.
- **Timeline-based tool:** align different kinds of evidence along common phases.
- **Flowchart-based tool:** keep branching clinical pathways at the centre instead of forcing them into a linear timeline.

Expert feedback raised a deeper question: **who decides which dimensions matter, and what the grades mean?** The thesis proposed jointly configuring the tool as part of the conversation. That was a future direction, not an implemented or longitudinally validated fourth thesis prototype.

## Explore the current web prototype

The later web version makes this direction tangible through **Set up together**: teams can define their purpose, adjust dimensions and weights, and explain their grading criteria. The workspace, evidence matrix, and discussion-priority views offer different ways into the same material. Projects stay in the browser and can be exchanged as JSON files.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pathway-redesign/setup-together.png" alt="Set up together view with editable experience-design and clinical-managerial dimensions, weights, and grading criteria" caption="Joint configuration in the current web prototype, distinct from the earlier thesis proposal. Measures and grading rules are choices to discuss, not universal clinical standards." />

This is a design and facilitation prototype, not a deployed hospital system or a clinical decision tool. Its historical pathway example demonstrates the interface, not current medical guidance or evidence of improved patient outcomes.

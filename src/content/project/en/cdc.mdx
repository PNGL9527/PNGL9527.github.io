## Background & Problem

### Project Background

Micro and small enterprises account for over 99% of all companies in Italy, yet they are often the most vulnerable when facing technological, regulatory, and market changes. While the Milan Chamber of Commerce (CdC) already provides information and services, existing support mechanisms tend to be fragmented and difficult to translate into actionable practice.

This project explored how the CdC could reposition training as a strategic service—not merely as knowledge transfer, but as a structured, participatory process that helps companies build confidence, experiment with change, and develop long-term resilience.

### Research & Framing

The project was framed around understanding how training services could better support micro and small enterprises in dealing with change. Rather than focusing solely on content delivery, the research examined training as a service system involving institutional actors, external partners, and participating companies.

The work combined analysis of the Chamber’s existing service structure with an exploration of small business needs, focusing on how practical learning, simulation, and collaboration could reduce resistance to change and increase engagement.

### Key Insights

• Small and micro enterprises do not primarily lack information; they lack confidence and practical ways to engage with change. Training therefore needs to be experiential rather than purely instructional.

• Learning is most effective when embedded in collective activities such as simulations, group work, and shared reflection, rather than individual classroom settings.

• Spatial design plays a critical role in shaping participation, enabling transitions between private learning, collaborative work, and public sharing.

### Service Concept: A Training Network

The proposal introduces a training service ecosystem coordinated by the Milan Chamber of Commerce. A dedicated Surveying and Forecasting Lab identifies emerging trends and translates them into sector-specific training programs, developed in collaboration with universities, experts, and industry partners.

Training is delivered through a combination of digital touchpoints, physical toolkits, and spatially orchestrated experiences, guiding companies from initial exploration to shared outcomes and long-term support.

## Design Concept

Based on the interview, we define the target group as micro and small companies, which have less than 50 employees. Since they are too small, sometimes they are not able to deal with some documentary formalities.

### Training Service: The Toolkit and the App

In our design concept, CdC also offers the training service for small businesses, facilitated by a set of tools and the App.

#### The Toolkit

<figure class="m-0">
  <LightboxImage
    Src={`${import. Meta. Env. BASE_URL}assets/projects/cdc/cdc_kits-4. Jpg`}
    Alt="The service kits part of the proposed new roles of the CdC"
    ClassName="rounded-2 xl w-full block"
  />
  <figcaption class="mt-2 text-sm leading-snug text-neutral-500  text-center">
    The service toolkit facilitating the Service role of the CdC.
  </figcaption>

With the service toolkit, CdC can offer training for small businesses with 4 directions:
1. **Open your mind:** training based on *the Challenge Canvas Guide.*
2. **Open your eyes:** training based on ***Case study (PEST and SWOT)*** and ***Scorecard System.***
3. **Open to innovation:** training based on *New Challenge Canvas*, *Risk Management*, and ***Strategic Action Plan***.
4. **Open to society:** training based on *Idea List* tool.

## Reflection

This project strengthened my ability to design at an organizational and systemic level, balancing institutional constraints with participatory learning experiences. It highlighted how service design can align policy, space, and interaction to support long-term behavioral change rather than short-term efficiency.

## Bringing care closer to rural communities

MediCall explores how women in rural Bangladesh could access medical consultation without a long journey to a hospital. Developed during a **one-week sustainable business design workshop**, the proposal connects a social need with an existing community network rather than starting with a new clinic.

The starting point was the local presence of **Grameen Bank meeting centres**. The concept asks whether these familiar locations could also host a point of access to remote care.

## A consultation point within the community

The proposed kiosk brings together **video consultation, basic measurement tools and medicine collection**. Solar power and mobile data were included to reduce reliance on fixed infrastructure, while a network of doctors would provide consultations remotely.

## A social purpose with an operating model

The model connects **women seeking care, doctors and local pharmacies**. Pharmacies would lease and stock the machines; the MediCall operator would support the hardware and platform, with rental income intended to sustain the service. The proposal also identifies mobile connectivity, manufacturing and software as supporting relationships.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/medicall/business-model.webp" alt="Original MediCall business model canvas showing proposed partners, activities, resources, value propositions, customer groups, costs and income model" caption="The workshop's business model canvas. Named organisations are proposed operating partners, not partnerships secured by the project." />

The key idea is to combine **local presence with remote expertise**. This remains a workshop proposal: the service, medical outcomes and financial viability were not validated through an operational pilot.

## A museum as a memory rock

This graduation design proposes a museum of local revolutionary history in Jinzhai, within the mountainous landscape of western Anhui. The brief brought together a commemorative role, public education, and the area's cultural-tourism development.

My starting point was a spatial tension: **how can a museum become a recognizable landmark while belonging to its hillside?** The proposal develops a single, rock-like body, then cuts and opens it to form exhibition spaces, a central public square, and connections to the surrounding landscape.

The architectural idea is a **memory rock**: history is held within a solid mass, while people move through it in the present. The project is presented here as a design proposal through its site studies, form development, plans, and updated architectural visualizations.

## Cultural background: memory and everyday life

The original project research explored Jinzhai through four cultural themes: revolutionary memory, local food, landscape tourism, and regional arts. Together, these offered a broader context for the museum than commemorative history alone.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/cultural-background.png" alt="Original cultural research board combining revolutionary heritage, local food and tea, mountain tourism, traditional architecture, and community celebrations in Jinzhai" caption="Cultural background research from the original graduation project: revolutionary heritage, food, tourism, and regional arts." />

### Revolutionary memory

The research foregrounded Jinzhai's association with revolutionary history through memorials, exhibitions, and collective commemorations. This theme informed the museum's role as a place for remembrance and public education, and the later idea of a building that holds history as a layer of memory.

### Food and the mountain landscape

The food references connect local identity to everyday life: tea cultivation, preserved foods, and regional ingredients. Alongside them, the tourism references show wooded mountains, rivers, and outdoor recreation. Read together, they situate the museum within an inhabited landscape of work, leisure, and visits, supporting the proposal's attention to terraces, walking routes, and public space.

### Regional arts and shared traditions

Traditional buildings, masonry details, festivals, and performances formed another strand of the research. The original board connected these references to Jinzhai's position near the meeting of Anhui, Henan, and Hubei and to histories of migration. For the proposal, they broadened the cultural frame to include built heritage and shared social practices alongside formal commemoration.

These themes helped frame the design question: how could a museum give revolutionary memory a recognizable architectural presence while remaining connected to the landscape and the everyday life around it?

## Read the site: between an old town and a new center

The site lies in Meishan, Jinzhai County. The urban context places historic places and the old town to the west and southwest, while the newer county center lies to the north. That contrast became a starting point for thinking about history, the present, and the future through the building's orientation.

### Access and a changing ground

The surrounding urban roads connect the site to the town and regional transport. Within the park, routes follow a more irregular mountainous terrain: lower fields, rising terraces, and wooded slopes create different conditions for arrival and movement.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/site-access.jpg" alt="Site access analysis distinguishing urban roads, internal routes, and connections to the town and surrounding region" caption="Access analysis: connect an identifiable arrival to the existing road and path network." />

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/site-pavilion-view.jpg" alt="Original site panorama viewed from the Red Army Pavilion" caption="Reading the wider landscape from the Red Army Pavilion." />

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/site-terrace-view.jpg" alt="Original site panorama viewed from the agricultural terraces" caption="At ground level, terraces reveal the site's sequence of changing heights." />

The decision was to work with the hillside rather than place the whole building on the flatter field. The terraces could then shape the approach, the subdivision of the volume, and the way the museum is seen from outside.

## Form the concept: landmark, history, and geological layers

Two ideas guided the early studies. **Landmark** called for a unified, memorable body. **Commemoration** called for weight, solemnity, and a place to encounter history. Rock and geological strata offered a way to bring these together.

The time diagrams distinguish the long passage of history from the shorter span of a person's visit. A museum gives a material setting to that encounter: what is present today will itself become a layer of history.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/history-present-future.jpg" alt="Concept diagram connecting history, the present, and the future through macro and micro timelines" caption="Time as a conceptual thread through the museum experience." />

This metaphor became a form-making rule: retain a coherent outer body, but articulate its surface and internal spaces through cuts, folds, and openings.

## Shape the building with the terrain

Site conditions translated the concept into a more specific arrangement. The southern approach connects to the flatter land, while the northern side responds to the newer town. Different height zones help separate the auditorium and exhibition areas, with shared public space connecting them.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/site-constraints.jpg" alt="Site constraint diagram showing program zones, north-south orientation, surrounding terrain, and the southern approach" caption="Orientation, arrival, and changes in level become constraints for the building mass." />

The volume develops through six operations: establish a contour-related boundary; extrude the main body; respond to the north and south; split spaces with the terraces; articulate a triangulated skin; and open an inner square.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/massing-sequence.png" alt="Six-stage massing sequence from contour boundary and extrusion to terrace response, triangulated skin, and inner-square generation" caption="From a solid landmark to a building organized by cuts and a public center." />

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/masterplan.jpg" alt="Masterplan of the faceted museum, central openings, surrounding access loop, paths, and terraced hillside" caption="The masterplan brings the museum, arrival space, landscape, and surrounding routes together." />

The perimeter route provides access around the building. The public approach is considered alongside separate staff and service access, rather than asking every movement to share the main entrance.

## Organize public space around a central square

The **platform square** is the organizing space between three main program groups: exhibition, auditorium and book gallery, and business and administration. It allows these parts to remain distinct while still belonging to one building.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/function-layout-connections.png" alt="Combined function layout and connection diagrams showing exhibition spaces, auditorium and book gallery, business and administration, lobbies, and the central platform square" caption="Function layout and connections: the central square and lobbies link the museum’s main program groups. Enlarge to inspect the diagrams." />

Two lobbies form secondary centers for their surrounding spaces. The resulting organization is multi-centered: the square connects the whole, while the lobbies make the different functional groups easier to enter and navigate.



## Resolve the proposal through plans

The first-floor plan places the auditorium, book gallery, exhibition areas, and supporting functions around the platform square and courtyard. Their relationship to the slope is visible in the changing levels and approaches.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/floor-plans.png" alt="Combined first-, second-, and third-floor plans showing exhibition spaces, auditorium, book gallery, central square, courtyard, and supporting rooms" caption="First-, second-, and third-floor plans. Enlarge to inspect the drawings." />

The upper-floor drawings develop additional support rooms and leisure uses, including a souvenir shop and coffee bar. These are connected to the main organization without repeating the entire first-floor program.

These are the original thesis drawings, displayed without a fixed on-screen scale.

## Separate routes without separating the experience

Circulation studies distinguish the general visitor route, the proposed accessible route, and the professional visitor route. Each draws on the same spatial structure but reaches it through different entrances and connections.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/circulation-plans.png" alt="Combined circulation diagrams for general visitors, accessible visitors, professional visitors, leisure spaces, exhibition installation, administration, and service routes" caption="Visitor, leisure, installation, administration, and service routes. Enlarge to inspect the diagrams." />

Installation, administration, and service flows are considered separately from the public visit. The intention is to let the museum function operationally while preserving a clear visitor sequence.

## The building in the landscape

The updated visualizations revisit the design proposal: a low, faceted body within a terraced hillside, with cuts in the roof and an open center that give the mass an internal public life.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/hillside-render.png" alt="Low, folded museum facade set into a terraced hillside beneath an overcast sky" caption="The museum's low profile follows the terraced hillside." />

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/arrival-render.png" alt="Museum arrival view showing the faceted stone body, glazed entrance, stepped landscape, and commemorative sculptures" caption="From the arrival space, the folded building reads as part of the larger landform." />

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/jinzhai-revolution-museum/courtyard-detail.png" alt="Close-up of the commemorative sculpture above broad courtyard steps, framed by folded stone walls and roof planes" caption="At the scale of a visit: steps, sculpture, and folded stone planes frame the commemorative space." />

The project connects a commemorative idea to architectural decisions at several scales: the museum's relationship to the town, its position on the slope, the shaping of its body, and the organization of rooms and routes around a public center.

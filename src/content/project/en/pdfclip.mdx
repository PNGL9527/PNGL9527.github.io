## A reader built around reading

When my own computer went back to China for repair, I worked on my wife's Mac. Finding a PDF Expert alternative that felt right proved unexpectedly difficult. I wanted to read, mark a passage, and keep going—not spend the session navigating a heavy interface.

That practical frustration became the brief for **PDFclip 2: keep the document at the centre, with tools close enough to use but quiet enough to forget.** It is a personal product-design and development project, built primarily with WorkBuddy.

The main reading surface leaves room for the page. Navigation and supporting tools can be brought into the session without becoming its permanent focus.

## Tools within reach, not always in the way

Annotation begins with the passage rather than a trip to a toolbar. Selecting text brings a compact set of actions into context, including markup and colour choices. The intention is to shorten the interruption between noticing something and leaving a useful mark.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pdfclip/02-quick-annotation.webp" width={2400} height={1825} alt="A selected thesis passage with PDFclip's contextual annotation menu and colour choices, alongside the document outline" caption="Selection-based actions bring annotation to the passage being read." />

The palette can also sit horizontally above the page or vertically at its side. These are alternative arrangements for different documents and reading positions, not additional panels that must all remain open.

Markup is stored as PDF annotations rather than only in a separate application-specific sidecar. The aim is for the reading work to remain connected to the document itself.

## Follow a thought without losing your place

Academic reading often moves between an argument and the material it points to. A linked reference can be previewed beside the originating passage, reducing the need to jump away and reconstruct the starting point.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pdfclip/04-reference-preview.webp" width={2400} height={1870} alt="A linked citation in the thesis with its bibliography target displayed in a floating page-preview panel" caption="A reference preview keeps the source passage and its linked destination together." />

For a longer comparison, two documents can share the window. In this example, my thesis sits beside a related paper. Each remains a readable document rather than a small thumbnail or an item hidden behind another tab.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pdfclip/05-side-by-side.webp" width={2400} height={1870} alt="PDFclip displaying my doctoral thesis and a related research paper in two adjacent document panes" caption="Side-by-side reading supports comparison without switching between windows." />

## Support around the page

Some reading sessions need more than markup. An optional AI sidebar allows a selected passage to become the context for a question and follow-up, while the document stays visible. It is a supporting conversation, not a replacement for reading or checking the source.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pdfclip/06-ai-assistance.webp" width={2400} height={1870} alt="PDFclip's AI sidebar beside a selected passage, showing a question about ADAS and follow-up answers in Chinese and English" caption="A selected passage and follow-up questions share the workspace; this example uses an OpenAI model." />

A page overview offers a different scale of attention: step back from the current paragraph and see the document's overall sequence. It gives navigation and page-management tasks their own space instead of crowding the everyday reading view.

<LightboxImage src="/assets/projects/pdfclip/07-page-overview.webp" width={2400} height={1870} alt="PDFclip's page overview displaying a grid of thesis-page thumbnails, with an Edit control above" caption="The page overview shown in viewing mode. Editing is a separate action rather than a permanent layer over the document." />

## Building a quieter tool

The **“2” marks a fresh start**. I first made a version based on an open-source PDF reader. That foundation was not written in SwiftUI, so I built a second version from scratch rather than continuing to adapt it. The current application uses SwiftUI, AppKit, and PDFKit.

I used WorkBuddy as the primary development partner, with my role centred on the product brief, interaction choices, and repeated refinement of the experience. Rebuilding the application gave me a new foundation for shaping the reader around the experience I wanted.

The guiding question is not how many features can fit in a reader, but **how little interface is needed for the next reading action**. Contextual markup, a repositionable palette, and reference previews are different answers to that same question.

PDFclip 2 remains in development and has not been publicly released. The screenshots show the running application with my own thesis and research paper; the original interface language is preserved.
